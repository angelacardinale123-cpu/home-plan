How to clean

Pick a room. Tick the steps as you go, so you never lose your place.

Kitchen

About twenty minutes.

What you needDish soap, sponge, all-purpose spray, a cloth, broom, a fresh trash bag.
Done looks likeCounters clear and dry, sink empty, no crumbs on the floor.

Bathroom

About twenty minutes.

What you needToilet cleaner, toilet brush, spray, paper towels, glass cleaner, a clean hand towel.
Done looks likeNo streaks on the mirror, nothing on the counter that does not live there, dry floor.

Living room

About ten minutes.

What you needVacuum, spray, a dry cloth, a basket for strays.
Done looks likeFloor clear, and nothing on the surfaces except what lives there.

Dining area

Three things daily, two more each week.

Done looks likeNothing on the table except what lives there.

Laundry

Your own clothes. Not a shared job.

What you needDetergent, your hamper, and a timer on your phone.
Done looks likeBoth machines empty, and nothing left in a basket overnight.

The one shared rule: do not leave your things in the washer or dryer overnight, because it blocks the other person. If you find their load sitting there, text them before you move it.

Your bedroom

Once a week.

This is your room, and how tidy you keep it in between is your call. The only part that affects the other person is dishes and trash, because that is what brings smells and bugs into the rest of the place.

House rules

  1. Clean up your own mess the same day.
  2. If you use the last of something, add it to the list right then.
  3. Nobody nags. Ask once, plainly, and leave it there.
  4. A missed week is just a missed week. Nobody keeps score.
  5. If something is not working, say so at the weekly reset.

If the split feels unfair

Give it a month first, because most jobs feel worse before they become a habit. If it still feels lopsided, trade the job you like least.
